What is the EHS ITO Process? (Rev 09)

The EHS (Environment, Health & Safety) ITO Process is GE Vernova's framework for identifying, assessing, and mitigating health, safety, and environmental risks during the Inquiry-to-Order (ITO) tendering stage — before a project is awarded. EHS workflow is mandatory for ALL opportunities which involve at least one GE employee, contractor, agent or other person hired on behalf of GE who will be sent to the customer site. Rather than being a separate standalone process, EHS is fully embedded within the SPEAK4GE RAM (Risk Assessment & Management) workflow. The level of EHS scrutiny required escalates based on deal size and country risk level — deals in High or Very High Risk countries require additional documentation, register entries, and approval from senior EHS leaders. All preparations, mitigations, and checklist outputs must be documented in the GA EHS ITO Checklist and uploaded to SPEAK4GE Chatter for record-keeping.

EHS Key Principles

5 Core Principles

  1. Safety First: All project activities must prioritize personnel safety and environmental protection.
  2. Risk Assessment: Comprehensive EHS risk identification and assessment at project initiation.
  3. Mitigation Planning: Develop and implement mitigation measures for identified risks.
  4. Compliance: Ensure adherence to local, regional, and GE Vernova EHS standards and regulations.
  5. Documentation & Escalation: Maintain complete EHS documentation and escalate high-risk items per tier requirements.

Brownfield Definition — PL DD2 Qualifier

What is Brownfield?

A Brownfield deal involves working on or upgrading an existing, already-operational system — as opposed to a Greenfield deal which starts from scratch on a new installation. In the Grid Automation context, Brownfield typically means modifying, extending, or replacing components within a live substation or network environment where existing equipment, systems, and third-party integrations are already in place. This introduces additional complexity around site access, system interoperability, data collection, migration planning, and responsibility boundaries — all of which must be carefully assessed during the tendering stage. The Brownfield definition below determines whether your deal qualifies for a PL DD2 Review (TKS classification) or falls under the Standard category, and applies to all deals with a Selling Price above $500k.

Interactive Qualifier

Q1: Is this a TKS Direct Bid or TKS through EPC/Channel Partner?

Q2: Does the scope include considerable Non-GE IED/System modification works exceeding 50% of Cost of Sales?

Q3: Is the work based on GE installed base standard modifications, standard engineered solutions, or loose products supply per established DOR?

Full Reference Section

BROWNFIELD TKS (Qualifies for PL DD2)

  • TKS Direct Bid: GE Vernova directly bids on TKS (Turnkey System) scope with full responsibility for system integration and delivery.
  • TKS through EPC/Channel Partner: GE Vernova supplies equipment/software as part of an EPC or channel partner's TKS delivery.
  • Non-GE Modifications >50% COS: Scope includes considerable modifications to non-GE IEDs or systems exceeding 50% of Cost of Sales.
  • Refurbishment of Competitor/Own Installed Base (DCS System Replacement): Requires Site Survey + COE Validation (JIRA). Classified as a Technical Risk trigger.
  • Refurbishment Scope (Execution Risk): Requires SME cost validation + Site survey data. If survey not available, sign off by Region Operation Leader is required.

BROWNFIELD STANDARD (Does NOT Qualify for PL DD2)

  • GE Installed Base Standard Works: Modifications to existing GE systems using established, proven procedures.
  • Standard Engineered Solution (per DOR): Engineered solutions based on Design of Record (DOR) with no novel technical elements.
  • Loose Products Supply (per DOR): Supply of individual products/components per established DOR without system integration complexity.
  • CIC Frame Agreement (no tech risk): Supply of CIC equipment (Infinera, Nokia, OTN, Huawei) under frame agreement with no technical or operational risk.
  • Standard Modifications: Routine modifications with no novel technical complexity or execution risk.
Important Note: Other criteria including Offshore/Wind/HVDC, APM, Technical Risks and respective proceedings remain intact. The above Brownfield definitions apply only to deals with Selling Price > $500k.
